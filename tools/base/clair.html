The chord map

The strip above the piano shows the section being played; this is the whole piece. Each card is one bar: three beats, each three eighth notes long (count “1-and-a, 2-and-a, 3-and-a”). A chord's width shows how long it lasts. Tap a chord to hear it and jump there; tap “play” on a card to start from that bar.

How to practise it

  1. Start with section C, bars 27–36. It is the part everyone knows, and each bar is the same three chords: D♭, F minor, D♭ minor. Loop it with Melody off and Block chords on.
  2. Then the opening, bars 1–8. The right hand is only two notes at a time. In the left hand, follow the bass note after the slash: it steps down one note per bar.
  3. Add the melody slowly. Speed 50–60, loop two bars. Follow the gold notes as they reach the keys. Many are held a long time, so there is room to think.
  4. Listen to Debussy's own left hand. Switch the chord style to hear how he spreads each chord into a wave of single notes. It is the same harmony you already know.
  5. Take section B in two halves. Bars 15–18 sit on E♭ minor. In bars 19–24 only the bass changes much: A♭, A, B♭, C, D♭, E♭.
  6. Leave section D for last. It is written in sharps and moves fastest. The chords are close cousins of the ones before: C♯ minor is D♭ minor under another name.

Reading the symbols

D♭/F
A slash chord: play a D♭ chord with F as the lowest note. The letter after the slash is the bass.
E♭m7
A minor seventh: minor triad plus a minor seventh (E♭ – G♭ – B♭ – D♭). The soft, hazy sound of this piece.
A♭7
A dominant seventh. It wants to resolve to D♭. A♭7sus swaps the third (C) for the fourth (D♭) first.
C°7
A diminished seventh: four notes stacked in minor thirds.
Cm7♭5
A half-diminished chord: a minor seventh chord with the fifth lowered.
A♭+
An augmented triad: a major triad with the fifth raised a half step.
I, V⁷, ii⁷
Roman numerals count chords from D♭ (from E in bars 37–42). Upper case is major, lower case is minor.
