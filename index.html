How to practise it

  1. Learn the eight chords of bars 1–4. Loop section A with Melody off and Block chords on. Play each chord with your left hand as it arrives. Those four bars come back four times, so they are half the piece.
  2. Hum the tune over them. Turn Melody on and listen to how each gold note sits on its chord. Most long melody notes are chord tones.
  3. Add the right hand slowly. Speed 50–60, loop one bar. Follow the gold notes as they reach the keys.
  4. Switch to the nocturne pattern. Bass note on each beat, then the chord twice. It is the same harmony, spread out, and it gives the rocking 12/8 feel.
  5. Move on to B and C. B has the quick chord-per-eighth slide in bar 12; loop it alone. C leans on A♭ minor, the darker colour of the ending.
  6. Treat the cadenza as free time. In bar 32 hold B♭7, let the run float, then land on E♭.

Reading the symbols

E♭
A plain letter is a major triad: root, major third, fifth (E♭ – G – B♭).
Fm, A♭m
“m” is a minor triad: the middle note is a half step lower.
B♭7
A dominant seventh: major triad plus a minor seventh. It wants to resolve, usually down a fifth (B♭7 → E♭).
D°7
A diminished seventh: four notes stacked in minor thirds. A tense, passing colour.
B♭7sus
The third (D) is replaced by the fourth (E♭), then resolves.
I, V⁷, ii
Roman numerals count chords from E♭. Upper case is major, lower case is minor. V⁷/ii means “the V⁷ of ii”, a chord borrowed to point at F minor.
